Inheritance

What is ADA Deficiency?

ADA deficiency is a rare inherited immune-system disorder. Changes in the ADA gene on chromosome 20 reduce or eliminate production of adenosine deaminase, an enzyme needed for healthy lymphocyte development and function.

Protein Synthesis

Transcription

DNA is packaged into chromosomes in the nucleus. The DNA stays in the nucleus for protection. A gene is a DNA instruction; during transcription, the ADA gene's instruction is copied into messenger RNA (mRNA) to be brought out of the nucleus.

Translation

Outside the nucleus, ribosomes read mRNA in groups of three bases at a time which form a codon. Each codon corresponds to a specific amino acid. The ribosome then joins amino acids in the specified order. This process builds a protein.

Protein

The amino acid sequence guides how a protein folds. The ADA protein—adenosine deaminase—normally breaks down adenosine and deoxyadenosine; its three-dimensional shape is essential to that job as the enzyme's active site must fit the molecules it acts on. ADA deficiency can result from a mutation that changes the amino acid sequence, altering the protein's abiltity to reduce the toxins.

Mutation

What Is a Mutation?

A mutation is a change in a gene's DNA sequence. A point mutation changes one DNA building block and can change one amino acid or stop protein production early. A frameshift mutation adds or removes bases, shifting the reading pattern for later amino acids.

Action of ADA

Adenosine deaminase normally breaks down adenosine and deoxyadenosine, helping prevent toxic breakdown products from building up.

Effect of Mutation

ADA mutations can make too little working enzyme or an enzyme that does not work properly. Harmful metabolites then accumulate, damaging lymphocytes and weakening immune defenses.

Symptoms

Frequent Infections

Reduced immune defenses can lead to repeated, severe, or unusual infections.

Chronic Diarrhea

Persistent diarrhea may occur alongside infections and can contribute to dehydration or poor nutrition.

Poor Growth

Frequent illness and difficulty absorbing enough nutrition can slow growth or cause failure to thrive.

Respiratory Illness

Susceptibility to pneumonia and other lung infections can make breathing problems serious.

Treatments

Enzyme Replacement

PEG-ADA supplies a modified form of the missing enzyme to lower toxic metabolites. It provides ADA activity while treatment continues, but it does not repair the ADA gene.

Stem Cell Transplantation

A hematopoietic stem cell transplant can replace blood-forming cells with donor cells that make functional ADA. When successful, it can restore ADA function in the immune system; donor availability and transplant risks matter.

Gene Therapy

Gene therapy adds a working ADA gene to a person's own blood-forming stem cells before they are returned. Its goal is to restore ADA function in immune cells without a donor transplant.

Supportive Care

Preventing and treating infections, using immune support when needed, and monitoring nutrition help protect health. Supportive care manages complications but does not restore ADA enzyme function.

Punnett Square

ADA deficiency is autosomal recessive: A represents a working copy and a a nonworking copy. The square combines one allele from each parent to show possible—not guaranteed—outcomes for each pregnancy.
